Publication summary · Sports Medicine · 2026

Athlete travel, sleep and recovery

A HERO coauthored review brings together research and expert guidance for planning athlete travel, with evidence that varies across topics.

Read the open full textDOI record

The question

What can research tell athletes and support staff about planning for long-haul travel?

How the research was done

This narrative review used a targeted, non-systematic literature search through August 2025. It generated no new participant data. The authors brought together research and expert guidance across sleep, circadian timing, hydration, fueling, illness, and recovery.

What the review contributes

The review offers a planning framework, not a tested travel intervention. It distinguishes jet lag from travel fatigue and identifies areas where athlete-specific evidence remains limited.

What this does and does not tell us

  • It does not establish that a particular travel plan improves match performance. Findings differ across domains and populations.
  • The literature search was non-systematic, so this is not an exhaustive evidence review.
  • A direct travel–injury link remains unestablished. Evidence on chronotype and adaptation is inconsistent, and routine probiotic use for travel is not supported.

Funding and disclosures

No funding was reported for the article. Kristen E. Holmes, Gregory J. Grosicki, Finnbarr Fielding, Christopher J. Chapman, and Josh Leota disclosed WHOOP employment, salaries, and stock options. The full declarations also describe separate author research support.
