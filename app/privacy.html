Privacy and your choices

Your plan. Your data choices.

Effective October 30, 2026.

HERO is operated by Jeremy Swisher. This notice describes the HERO app and its public app information pages. For support or privacy questions, contact jswisher@mednet.ucla.edu.

Plans on your device and in an account

Without an account, HERO stores your workspace on your device. It includes your goals and usual schedule, current and saved weeks, commitment titles and times, sleep-opportunity target, templates, routines, saved evidence, recovery commitments and optional manual check-ins.

If you create an account, Supabase manages your sign-in, account profile, account identifier and session. You can sign in with an email address and password on the web and in the iOS app. Where offered, you can instead choose Continue with Google or Continue with Apple. On the web, each opens the provider's own sign-in page. In the iOS app, Apple uses the system Sign in with Apple sheet, and Google opens in a secure browser sheet. HERO saves your separate account workspace through Supabase, including the sleep, training, energy, soreness and recovery information you enter manually. This health and fitness information is account-linked when saved in your account workspace. It differs from imported Apple Health and WHOOP measurements, which stay in session memory as described below.

HERO uses this information to provide accounts, workspace storage and sync, planning tools, saved items, security and support. Passwords go to Supabase Auth and are not stored in your plan. Resend delivers authentication emails and processes the recipient address, message content and related delivery information. Your workspace and imported health summaries are not included in those emails.

In versions offering Continue with Google, choosing it shares your Google provider identifier, email address and basic profile information with Supabase Auth. This information can include your name and a URL for your profile photo. Supabase Auth can retain these details in your account profile. HERO does not fetch or display the profile photo. Your Google password is not given to HERO.

In versions offering Continue with Apple, choosing it shares an app-specific identifier and the email address you choose to share. If you choose Hide My Email, that is a private relay address that forwards to your email, and HERO does not receive your own address. Supabase Auth retains the identity with your HERO account. Native iOS sign-in requests email, not your full name. Apple web sign-in can also supply profile details, such as a name if Apple returns it, that Supabase Auth can retain with your account.

Google and Apple sign-in identify the HERO account to open. They do not grant calendar or health access or subscribe your address to a newsletter. Calendar and health connections require separate choices.

Native sign-in credentials use device secure storage, and reinstalling the iOS app starts signed out. On web, the HERO sign-in session is stored for the browser tab. If you add HERO to your home screen or install it as an app from your browser, that installed copy keeps you signed in on that device until you sign out of HERO there or delete your account, or until HERO's service no longer accepts the sign-in. Closing a tab ends that session but leaves the account workspace copy, its backups and any saved answers in the browser. On a shared computer, sign out and then clear this site's data in your browser settings. Native Apple sign-in also keeps the Apple identifier and matching HERO account and session identifiers in secure storage to respond when Apple sign-in permission is revoked. After Apple web sign-in, HERO can retain an Apple refresh credential in that browser tab or app window until it closes, so account deletion can request revocation. An installed copy that keeps you signed in does not keep this credential after it closes. Signing out or deleting the account attempts to clear these local Apple records.

Your guest and account workspaces are separate. Signing out clears the local sign-in session, removes saved answers and returns you to the guest workspace. The account workspace copy and its backups stay on the device until you delete the account from that device. Signing out does not delete your Supabase Auth profile, including your email address, account and provider identifiers, and any profile details supplied during sign-in.

Manual check-ins and weekly review

Check-ins are optional self-reports of estimated sleep, energy and soreness. HERO summarizes recorded days and keeps missing days separate. A weekly review does not establish that a routine caused a change or make a medical assessment. These entries follow the same device storage, account sync and deletion rules as your workspace.

Your 4-week record and summaries you share

In versions with the 4-week record, Weekly review can show a record that HERO builds on your device from your saved weeks, check-ins and commitments, with the weekly focus marks and check-in entry times your device saved. It never includes wearable measurements. Hiding nightly numbers is a setting kept only on your device.

In versions that offer summaries, Prepare a summary builds a text summary on your device from the dates and sections you choose, plus any answers to its sleep questions and any note you add. That note and those answers are not saved on your device. When it includes your commitments, it also lists each weekly focus with how often you marked it done on your device. When you share, download or print a summary yourself, HERO never receives it and keeps no record of who receives it. Summaries never include wearable measurements.

In versions that offer share links, if you create one, HERO stores a copy of the summary as it reads at that moment, including any note and sleep question answers in it, so the person you send the link to can open it in a browser without a HERO account. The copy never includes wearable measurements, your email address or account identifiers. The link works for the 7, 14 or 30 days you choose, and you can turn it off at any time, which deletes HERO's copy. HERO stops showing an expired copy and deletes it in routine cleanup. HERO counts how many times a link is opened and when it was last opened, and does not record who opened it, their IP address or their device. As with any web request, HERO's hosting services can receive the IP address and device details of whoever opens a link. A label you give a link is shown only to you. For 30 days after a link ends, its dates, label and count stay with your account. Anyone who has the link can open it until it ends, so send it only to medical staff you choose.

In versions that offer the weekly recap, if you turn it on, each Monday HERO's server reads your synced plans and check-ins and emails you a summary of last week: how many nights your plan made room for your sleep target, how many days you checked in, the range of sleep you reported and how many of those nights reached your target, plus one suggestion for the week ahead. These self-reported numbers pass through Resend, HERO's email provider, to reach you, and Resend may keep a copy under its own policies. HERO keeps only that you opted in, your time zone, when the last recap was sent and an error code if one could not be sent, never the email or its numbers. Apple Health and WHOOP data are never included. Turn it off in HERO or with the unsubscribe link in any recap.

Share only with medical staff you choose. You never have to share with coaches. Once shared, the recipient's organization's privacy rules apply to their copy, for example a school's student records rules.

Settings kept on your device

In newer versions, some features keep their settings only on your device, for the workspace that is open: your athlete profile and its sleep question answer, competition details on events you mark as competitions, saved trips, weekly focus marks, reminder settings, days you mark as sick, injured or rest days, new-week choices, the hide nightly numbers choice and the time each check-in was entered (which lets your record and summary mark late entries), which getting started steps and first-visit tips you have seen, recovery playbooks you mark as active, optional body and mind detail you add after a check-in (sore areas, stress and mood), removed with its check-in, the intensity you choose for training blocks, the supplement list on What I take (names, brands and when you take them), personal experiments you try (the change, its dates and length). These settings are not synced to your account or sent to HERO. They stay on the device with your workspace copy, and deleting your account from that device removes them.

Your athlete profile holds your sport, season and usual practice and lift times. Competition details hold home or away, warm-up time, whether the event is a key competition, a linked saved trip and an optional opponent name. A saved trip holds what you enter in the travel planner, such as dates, times, time zones, sleep settings and an optional name. New-week choices record which weeks HERO started for you and, so a new week can be undone, copies of any weeks it set aside.

In versions that start new weeks for you, HERO first saves a backup of the workspace on your device and keeps only the most recent one. If you allow HERO to retire your oldest saved week at the 12-week limit, it can also keep up to 9 backups that hold your saved weeks, so a retired week can be restored. Backups stay on the device until you remove them in Backups or delete your account from that device.

In versions with reminders, reminders are off until you turn them on. In the installed app, they are local notifications scheduled on your device, and HERO asks for notification permission only when you turn one on. HERO does not use push notifications or a push token, and reminder settings stay on your device. Reminder text stays general and does not include your check-ins or measurements. Each reminder is one of a few fixed lines. On the web, and on a phone where notifications for HERO are off or not yet allowed, reminders appear inside HERO instead. Reminders can follow the competitions you mark and pause on travel days, which HERO works out on your device from your saved trips and longer travel in your week.

On the web, if your browser installs HERO or keeps it for offline use, it stores only HERO's own code, fonts and images. If you dismiss the suggestion to add HERO to your home screen, that browser remembers the choice.

Calendars

Calendar access is optional. In the installed app, you choose a device calendar and review one week before importing selected busy intervals. Selected times become ordinary plan commitments with the generic title Calendar commitment, saved on your device and synced when you use an account. Calendar names, original event titles, source identifiers, guests, notes and locations are not saved in the HERO plan.

Newer versions also keep original imported start and end times with a commitment to compare later imports with your edits. This does not retain event identity or enable background access.

Web calendar-file import reads a chosen .ics file from Google Calendar, Apple Calendar or Outlook (when it uses common Windows time zones) in your browser without uploading it. Only selected busy times are saved. File names and original event details are not saved. This does not connect your Google account.

In versions with team calendar sync, a team calendar link you add is kept with your account, encrypted, along with the name you give it and the link's host, such as calendar.google.com. HERO's server reads the calendar when you add it, when you refresh it, and when you open HERO more than 6 hours after the last refresh, then passes it to your device without keeping a copy. Your device reads the events to add generic commitments, such as Team training or Team competition, and to suggest travel plans for flights. Event titles, notes, locations, guests and flight details are not saved or sent to Ask HERO. The calendar's host sees requests from HERO's server, not from your device. You can remove a team calendar at any time, and deleting your account deletes its saved links.

Travel planning uses the dates, time zones, sleep preferences and buffers you enter on your device. In versions that save trips, building a travel preview or choosing Save trip keeps the trip details on your device only. They are not synced, and deleting your account from that device removes them. Earlier versions keep previews in memory only. Proposed windows stay in memory until you apply them. Applied windows become normal plan commitments and can sync to your HERO account. These calendar and travel details are not attached to Ask HERO questions.

Native export opens a calendar editor so you can review and save a HERO commitment. Web export creates a calendar file containing plan titles and times for you to import. These actions do not enable background or two-way sync. Later changes in the source calendar do not update imported HERO commitments.

Apple Health

Apple Health access is optional and read-only in a compatible installed HERO iOS app with Health access. When you choose to connect, HERO requests permission to read sleep analysis, resting heart rate, heart rate variability (SDNN), steps and workouts. HERO does not write Health records.

HERO reads recent measurements into summaries held in app memory for the current session. It does not upload them to its backend, save them in the device workspace, include them in account sync, send them to AI services, or use them for analytics, advertising or newsletters. The summaries are cleared when the app session ends, you change account or you clear the imported view.

The daily recovery brief displays these summaries beside the plan and optional check-in on your device. Sources, measurement dates and import times remain distinct. The brief does not combine them into a readiness score or send them to AI.

In versions with trends, Weekly review can show the last 7 days of these measurements beside your check-ins, worked out on your device from what you already imported. HERO does not save the trends, and it reads Apple Health again only when you choose Refresh.

Clearing imported data removes the displayed summaries without deleting original Health records or revoking permission. Change HERO's read access in Apple Health or Settings. Empty results can mean that no readable data is available and do not establish whether permission was granted or denied.

WHOOP in supported versions

In versions that support WHOOP, connecting it is optional and requires a HERO account. You choose whether to authorize read access through WHOOP for cycles, recovery, sleep and workouts. The offline permission lets an approved connection refresh its access token. HERO does not request your WHOOP profile or body measurements.

The HERO backend stores encrypted WHOOP access and refresh tokens, approved scopes and connection metadata in Supabase. When you choose to import WHOOP measurements, they pass through HERO's backend and are returned as summaries held in app memory for the current session. HERO does not save those measurements in its database or device workspace, include them in account sync, send them to AI services, or use them for analytics, advertising or newsletters. The summaries are cleared when the app session ends, you change account or you clear the imported view.

In versions with trends, Weekly review can show WHOOP measurements you imported as trends beside your check-ins. The trends are worked out on your device and are not saved, and HERO imports WHOOP data again only when you choose to.

Clearing imported data removes the session's summaries without deleting original WHOOP records or revoking access. Disconnect WHOOP attempts to revoke access and clears HERO's saved connection after WHOOP confirms revocation. If revocation fails, HERO keeps the connection so you can retry. You can also remove HERO's access directly in WHOOP's connected-app settings.

Ask HERO AI in supported versions

AI answers are available only in versions with AI enabled and require a signed-in HERO account.

Ask HERO asks for permission before each question or retry is sent through the HERO backend. The prompt shows your question, names OpenAI and links to the privacy notice. Canceling sends nothing. To prepare an AI answer, HERO sends your question and selected public evidence cards to OpenAI. It does not attach your account email or identifier, calendar plan, Apple Health measurements or WHOOP measurements. Information you type into the question is part of the submitted text, so avoid identifying information or sensitive medical details. Newer versions also tell the HERO backend which published evidence notes the app holds, so answers cite only notes you can open. These note identifiers are the same for everyone, and the list is not sent to OpenAI.

HERO may also send submitted questions and generated general answers to OpenAI's moderation service to check for safety concerns. Some requests may receive a fixed reply without an AI-generated answer.

HERO does not save questions or answers in its database. The answer-generation request disables response storage for later retrieval. That setting does not promise zero retention by OpenAI or infrastructure providers, and it does not establish the retention rules for moderation requests. Provider processing and retention depend on the applicable policies and service settings. Supabase may log platform request metadata, and HERO stores account-linked request timestamps to enforce usage limits.

In versions that offer saved answers, you may choose Save on this device for a general answer. This stores the question, answer, cited evidence identifiers and save time in ordinary local app or browser storage for your signed-in HERO account, with a limit of 20. It does not sync to HERO's database or another device. Remove individual answers or clear the collection in Ask HERO. Signing out or deleting the account attempts to clear the current device's saved answers; cleanup failures are shown for retry. Device and browser backups follow their own settings. Short safety guidance may be displayed locally without sending a request.

Asking an AI question does not contact the research team. Contacting the team remains a separate action that opens an email draft for you to review and send.

Evidence search, email and external links

Using Find related evidence in Ask HERO happens on your device. This local topic-matching action does not send the question to an AI service or HERO's backend. Saved evidence article identifiers are part of your workspace.

Contact actions open a draft in your email app. You review and send it yourself. In newer versions, drafts to the research team ask you not to include medical details. Support and research messages can contain your address, subject, message and any attachments you choose to send. HERO does not automatically email your plan or imported health summaries. Please do not send passwords, sign-in codes, access tokens or medical records.

Research links, HERO Brief archives and newsletter signup open external websites with their own privacy practices. Creating a HERO account does not subscribe your email address to a newsletter.

Research at HERO and content updates

In versions with Research at HERO, its pages describe studies using HERO's reviewed content. When a study's pre-screen is open, Check if you may qualify opens the study team's REDCap pre-screen in your browser. The link carries only a study code and a channel code. HERO sends and stores nothing else. The channel code says where you found the link: the app, an athletic trainer card, an information session or the HERO Brief. A form to hear about future HERO studies opens the same way, with nothing added. What you enter in REDCap is handled by the study team under its REDCap practices, and the HERO app does not receive it. HERO does not collect research answers or consent, and it does not track which study pages you open.

In versions that update content, HERO checks for new evidence notes, HERO Brief issues and study pages when it opens and from time to time while it is open. The check is anonymous. It does not include your account, workspace, check-ins or measurements, and HERO adds no analytics to it. HERO keeps one copy of the latest content that passes its checks on your device. That copy holds only published content and is shared by every account on the device, so signing out or deleting your account does not remove it. Like any web request, the hosting service can receive your IP address and device details.

Retention and deletion

Your Supabase Auth profile remains until you delete your HERO account. Your saved account workspace remains until you replace it or delete the account. Local workspaces and backups can remain on a device. Imported Apple Health and WHOOP summaries last only for the current app session. WHOOP credentials remain while the connection is saved. Support correspondence is retained as needed to respond to requests, maintain security and meet legal obligations.

Hosting, authentication and email providers can retain operational logs and backups independently under their own policies and service settings. Deleting an account does not immediately erase those independent records or email correspondence.

To request permanent account deletion in HERO, open Your account > Delete my account, review the notice and confirm. After the server confirms deletion, the HERO account, its Supabase Auth profile and stored account workspace are removed, and in versions that offer share links, its links and their copies. The app then attempts to clear that account's workspace, backups, saved answers and saved sign-in on the current device, and in newer versions the settings kept only on that device. If cleanup fails, Your account offers Retry device cleanup. Other devices and browsers can retain offline copies until their local data is cleared. Your guest workspace stays separate.

In versions offering Apple sign-in, deleting an Apple-linked HERO account attempts to revoke its Apple sign-in authorization. On iOS, Apple may ask you to confirm your identity and HERO sends the fresh authorization code to its deletion service. Web deletion can instead send an available Apple refresh credential for that account. The service uses the credential for that deletion request to verify the Apple identity and request revocation. It does not save the credential in the HERO database or planner. Cancelling the Apple confirmation leaves your HERO account in place. You can expressly choose to delete HERO and remove its Apple sign-in connection yourself. If revocation cannot be confirmed, HERO account deletion can still finish, and the app provides instructions to remove HERO in your Apple Account settings.

Deleting your HERO account does not delete your Google or Apple account. To remove HERO's Google sign-in connection, remove HERO from the third-party apps and services connected to your Google Account, under Security. To remove HERO's Apple sign-in connection, open Sign in with Apple in your Apple Account settings (on iPhone: Settings, your name, Sign-In & Security) and remove HERO. Removing a sign-in connection does not delete your HERO account or end a web session that is already open, so sign out in HERO too. The iOS app signs you out when it finds that Apple permission was removed.

In versions that support AI answers or WHOOP, account deletion also removes HERO's request counters for those services. In versions that support WHOOP, it removes HERO's saved WHOOP credentials, connection metadata and pending connection requests. HERO attempts to revoke WHOOP access before deleting the account. If revocation cannot be confirmed, account deletion still removes those HERO records, and you should remove HERO directly in WHOOP's connected-app settings. Account deletion does not delete original WHOOP records.

Account deletion does not remove your original Apple Health records or revoke Health permissions. If you cannot access the account, or want to request access, correction or deletion of your information, contact jswisher@mednet.ucla.edu. We may ask for information needed to verify that the request concerns your account. Do not send your password or sign-in codes.

Service providers and advertising

HERO uses Supabase for authentication, account storage and the Ask HERO backend; Google and Apple for their optional sign-in methods in supported versions; OpenAI for AI answers and safety moderation in supported versions; Resend for authentication email delivery and, if you turn it on, the weekly recap; and hosting and email services for these pages and support correspondence. They process information needed to provide those services. Public-page hosting can receive request information such as IP addresses, browser details and access times.

The HERO app includes no advertising or analytics SDK, does not sell your personal information and does not use Apple Health data for advertising, marketing or data mining. HERO does not automatically publish your workspace or use it for cross-company advertising tracking.
