Sleep extension in NCAA athletes

A 10-hour nightly target and daily reminders did not significantly increase device-estimated sleep in this short, offseason study.

Read the open full textPubMed recordDOI record

The question

Can a nightly sleep target and daily reminders help collegiate athletes sleep longer?

How the research was done

At one university, 50 athletes from men's and women's soccer, baseball, and football completed two weeks of usual sleep followed by two weeks targeting 10 hours nightly. Student athletic trainers delivered daily reminders. WHOOP 4.0 bands estimated sleep during each sport's offseason. There was no separate control group.

Key finding

Nighttime sleep stayed near 7.6 to 7.5 hours, with no statistically significant increase (49 athletes with paired data). WHOOP's sleep consistency score rose from about 60% to 64% (46 athletes with paired data).

What this does and does not tell us

  • The fixed before-and-after sequence cannot establish that reminders caused the consistency change.
  • Sleep was estimated by WHOOP, not laboratory polysomnography. Naps were excluded, so this is not a measure of total 24-hour sleep.
  • The study was short and limited to one institution. Missing data, unequal sport samples, no prespecified primary outcome, and no adjustment for multiple testing further limit interpretation.
  • Athletic performance was not measured. These findings do not show that a 10-hour target improves performance, validate the device, or establish that sleep extension is generally ineffective.

Funding and disclosures

WHOOP provided grant funding, devices, data management, and technical and statistical support. Coauthors Jeongeun Kim and Kristen E. Holmes were WHOOP employees.

The original paper
