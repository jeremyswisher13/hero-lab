Publication summary · Clinical Journal of Sport Medicine · 2024

Baseline sleep in collegiate athletes

Wearable recordings and questionnaires describe sleep in a group of collegiate athletes. They do not establish a sleep target or validate the device.

Read the PubMed abstractPublisher and access options

The question

What were the baseline sleep patterns of male and female Division I student-athletes?

How the research was done

This prospective study followed 82 athletes at a Division I college. Participants wore WHOOP 4.0 bands for at least 14 nights and completed the Epworth Sleepiness Scale and Single-Item Sleep Quality Scale. The study described sleep rather than testing a sleep intervention.

Key finding

Device-estimated nighttime sleep averaged 409.2 minutes, approximately 6 hours 49 minutes. The study combined these recordings with measures of perceived sleep quality and daytime sleepiness.

What this does and does not tell us

  • The findings describe the athletes studied and do not establish population norms or an optimal sleep duration.
  • There was no laboratory polysomnography comparison. Sleep duration and stage measurements are device estimates; this study does not validate WHOOP 4.0.
  • An observational description cannot show that changing sleep, or following a wearable score, improves athletic performance.

Funding and disclosures

The PubMed record lists a WHOOP grant. Coauthors Kristen E. Holmes, David Presby, and Jeongeun Kim list WHOOP affiliations. The record's conflict-of-interest statement reports no conflicts.

The original paper

Baseline Sleep Characteristics in NCAA Division I Collegiate Athletes
