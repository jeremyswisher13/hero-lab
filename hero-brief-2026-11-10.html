What we're working on

From UCLA to SupplySide Global

On October 28, I spoke at SupplySide Global in Las Vegas about our UCLA magnesium L-threonate study in collegiate athletes. The four-week, randomized, placebo-controlled trial paired WHOOP monitoring with reaction-time and physical-performance testing to examine sleep and recovery. More about the study →

Big milestone this month for our magnesium trial in UCLA varsity athletes. We finished post-testing on November 6! The trial compares magnesium glycinate, magnesium L-threonate and placebo, and our main question is whether either one improves sleep efficiency, measured with WHOOP. We're working through the data now and will share what we find once the analysis is done. Huge thanks to our athletes and everyone who helped make it happen. Questions about the study? Contact our team →

New from our UCLA colleagues. HERO Lab co-PI Josh Goldman and colleagues at UCLA, UCSF, UC Irvine and WHOOP just published a sleep extension study in Sports Health. Fifty UCLA Division I athletes from women's soccer, men's soccer, baseball and football tracked their nighttime sleep with WHOOP for two weeks in the offseason. Then they were asked to aim for 10 hours a night for two more weeks, with daily reminders. Nighttime sleep did not significantly change (7.6 versus 7.5 hours), but WHOOP's sleep-consistency score improved (60% to 64%). In this small, single-school study, a sleep target with daily reminders did not increase sleep. There was no separate control group. Sports Health · October 2026 →

We're also preparing two reviews: magnesium use in athletes, and when traveling athletes should keep their home sleep schedule or shift to the destination time zone.

Three worth your time

1. Magnesium L-threonate: mixed results. A six-week randomized, double-blind trial of 100 adults dissatisfied with their sleep found modest benefits from magnesium L-threonate (Magtein) over placebo on an overall cognitive test and self-reported sleep-related impairment. Oura-estimated sleep measures and a separate reasoning test showed no clear difference between groups.

The sponsor helped design the trial; multiple secondary analyses warrant caution. Athletic performance was not tested. Frontiers in Nutrition · January 2026 → Publisher correction →

2. Later practice, more sleep. In a randomized crossover study, 25 adolescent swimmers contributed sleep data. They averaged one extra hour of sleep when morning training started at 9 instead of 7 a.m.: 6.9 versus 5.9 hours.

Swim performance and most perceived recovery measures did not clearly improve during the short camps. Sports Medicine–Open · July 2026 →

3. Cold water: timing matters. An umbrella review summarized 15 meta-analyses covering 161 unique studies of cold-water immersion after exercise. Cold water may ease soreness for up to four days. Power and jump height may recover faster a day or two later, but jump height can dip in the first few hours. The review found no clear short-term strength benefit, and repeated use right after lifting may blunt strength and muscle gains.

All of the underlying reviews were rated low or critically low quality. If you have another explosive effort in the next few hours, skip the tub. Sports Medicine–Open · September 2026 →

Worth a look

Nike Air Zoom Hyperslide: heat and vibration for your feet

What's new. Nike and Hyperice's Air Zoom Hyperslide combines Air Zoom cushioning with removable magnetic pods that deliver heat and vibration across the top of each foot. It launched September 29. Meet the Hyperslide →

Useful details. The U.S. price is $249 per pair, with availability varying by size and color. There are three heat levels, three vibration levels and 15-minute default sessions. Pod buttons handle basic controls; the Hyperice App adds timer customization and synchronizes the pods. Hyperice rates battery life at up to one hour with maximum heat and vibration. Price and availability → Specifications →

Our take. The appeal is warmth and convenience. We have not identified a controlled study of the Hyperslide showing faster recovery or improved athletic performance. Treat it as an optional comfort purchase.
