WHOOP, sleep and HRV

What wearable validation studies can tell us about athlete monitoring — and where uncertainty remains.

How accurate is WHOOP for sleep and HRV?

Accuracy depends on the device, algorithm, measurement, and testing conditions. A laboratory sleep study found better agreement for sleep versus wake than for individual sleep stages. The broader wearable review found that HRV agreement varied with conditions and was generally stronger at rest. Those results cannot be applied automatically to every WHOOP generation or recovery score.

The papers below address different questions: measurement accuracy, sleep patterns in athletes, and associations with health measures. A study using a wearable does not, by itself, validate the device or show that following its scores improves health or performance.

The studies and their limits

2020Journal of Sports Sciences

A validation study of the WHOOP strap against polysomnography to assess sleep

Miller DJ, Lastella M, Scanlan AT, et al.

In 12 healthy adults, researchers compared 86 sleep episodes over a 10-day laboratory protocol with polysomnography, a laboratory sleep test. WHOOP showed 89% sleep/wake agreement, 51% wake specificity, and 64% agreement across four categories: wake, light sleep, slow-wave sleep, and REM. Researchers manually entered bedtimes.

How to interpret it: The 89% figure concerns sleep versus wake, not 89% accuracy for every sleep stage. Wake specificity describes detection of time actually awake. This study did not validate HRV, a recovery score, or newer device generations.

Funding & disclosures: Australian Research Council and Australian Institute of Sport support. The publisher discloses WHOOP sponsorship of Miller’s research position beginning after manuscript submission, with a potential conflict for coauthors in the same research group.

PubMed Publisher / access options DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2020.1797448
2024Clinical Journal of Sport Medicine

Baseline Sleep Characteristics in NCAA Division I Collegiate Athletes

Goldman JT, Donohoe B, Hatamiya N, et al. — HERO Lab team publication

A prospective study followed 82 Division I athletes using WHOOP 4.0 for at least 14 nights, alongside sleep-quality and daytime-sleepiness questionnaires. Device-estimated total sleep time averaged 409.2 minutes, approximately 6 hours 49 minutes.

How to interpret it: These observations describe the athletes studied. They do not establish population norms or validate WHOOP 4.0 against polysomnography. Reported sleep stages are device estimates.

Funding & disclosures: PubMed lists a WHOOP grant and company-affiliated coauthors; its conflict statement reports no conflicts.

PubMed Publisher / DOI
2024International Journal of Sports Science & Coaching

Wearable technology metrics are associated with energy deficiency and psychological stress in elite swimmers

Lundstrom EA, De Souza MJ, Koltun KJ, et al.

In a cross-sectional study of 23 collegiate swimmers during heavy training, lower WHOOP heart rate variability was associated with metabolic suppression and greater reported stress. Findings differed by sex.

How to interpret it: This small study explored associations. It did not test whether WHOOP can diagnose or predict relative energy deficiency in sport, or show that changes in HRV cause changes in energy status. The device generation was not specified in the methods.

Funding & disclosures: The authors report no financial support and no potential conflicts.

Publisher Full-text PDF DOI: 10.1177/17479541231206424
2024Sports Medicine

Keeping Pace with Wearables: A Living Umbrella Review of Systematic Reviews

Doherty C, Baldwin M, Keogh A, Caulfield B, Argent R.

This review brought together 24 systematic reviews of consumer-wearable accuracy. Results varied across devices, measurements, and testing conditions. Sleep estimates often overestimated total sleep time, and HRV agreement was generally stronger at rest than during movement.

How to interpret it: The evidence covers multiple brands and generations, with different reference methods. It does not establish blanket accuracy for WHOOP, current products, or proprietary recovery scores. “Living” in the title does not mean the 2024 article evaluates every later model.

Funding & disclosures: Science Foundation Ireland National Challenge Fund support and IReL open-access support; the authors report no conflicts.

Open full text DOI: 10.1007/s40279-024-02077-2
